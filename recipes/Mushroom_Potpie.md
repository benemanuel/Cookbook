# Mushroom Potpie

*Source: New York Times Cooking*

## Ingredients

- ½ cup dried porcini mushrooms (1/2 ounce)
- 4 tablespoons olive oil, plus more as needed
- ½ pound fresh oyster mushrooms, torn into separate pieces (or shiitake mushrooms, stemmed and halved)
- Kosher salt and black pepper
- ½ pound cremini mushrooms, stemmed and quartered (or cut into sixths if large)
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1 large yellow onion, finely chopped
- ¼ pound lacinato kale, stemmed, leaves thinly sliced crosswise
- 4 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1 tablespoon fresh thyme leaves
- 2 teaspoons chopped fresh rosemary
- ¼ cup all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
- 1½ cups chicken or vegetable stock
- ¾ cup heavy cream
- 1 cup diced peeled carrots (from 2 medium carrots, cut into 1/2-inch pieces)
- 1 cup diced peeled parsnips (from 2 medium parsnips, cut into 1/2-inch pieces)
- 1 cup diced red potatoes (from 2 small potatoes, cut into 1/2-inch pieces)
- 1 (14- to 16-ounce) package puff pastry, thawed
- 1 large egg

## Instructions

1. Heat oven to 425 degrees and bring 2 cups of water to boil in a small pot.
2. Place the porcini mushrooms in a medium bowl and pour the boiling water on top; set aside to soak and soften.
3. In a large (12-inch) ovenproof skillet, heat 2 tablespoons oil over medium-high and working in two batches, add half the oyster mushrooms, season with salt and pepper and cook undisturbed until browned underneath, about 3 minutes.
4. Stir the oyster mushrooms and continue to cook until browned all over, about 3 minutes, then transfer to a medium bowl.
5. Repeat with 2 tablespoons oil and the remaining oyster mushrooms and transfer them to the medium bowl.
6. Add another 2 tablespoons oil, then add the cremini mushrooms, season with salt and pepper and cook, stirring occasionally, until browned and tender, 6 to 8 minutes, lowering the heat as needed to avoid burning, then transfer to the bowl.
7. Reduce the heat to medium and add the butter to the skillet, then once melted, add the onion and cook until starting to soften, about 5 minutes.
8. Add the kale, garlic, thyme and rosemary, season with salt and pepper and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened, about 3 minutes, adding 1 to 2 tablespoons oil if the pan seems dry.
9. Sprinkle with the flour and cook, stirring, until flour is dispersed and lightly toasted, 2 minutes.
10. Stirring constantly, gradually add the stock, cream and 3/4 cup of the liquid from the soaking porcini mushrooms.
11. Cook over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until thickened, about 5 minutes, then remove from heat.
12. Stir in the carrots, parsnips, potatoes, and the sautéed mushrooms and any accumulated juices.
13. Strain the soaked porcini mushrooms, discard any remaining liquid, then roughly chop the mushrooms and stir them into the filling.
14. Season to taste with salt and pepper.
15. On a lightly dusted work surface using a lightly floured rolling pin, roll out the puff pastry into a roughly 14-inch circle.
16. Transfer the puff pastry to the top of the skillet, allowing slight overhang, and fold under and pinch any overhang just below the rim of the skillet.
17. In a small bowl, whisk the egg with 1 teaspoon water and lightly brush the egg wash over the top of the puff pastry.
18. Cut 5 (2-inch) slits into the puff pastry, starting near the center and radiating outward like the rays of the sun, or create a stripe or crisscross pattern by gently scoring it with a paring knife without fully cutting through.
19. Crimp the edges using the tines of a fork if desired.
20. Set the skillet on top of an aluminum foil-lined baking sheet and transfer to the middle rack of the oven.
21. Bake until the puff pastry is puffed and golden, about 30 minutes, then let sit 10 minutes before serving.
