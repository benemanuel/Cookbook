# Seville Orange Marmalade

*Source: simplyrecipes.com*

## Ingredients

- 3 pounds Seville or bitter oranges (6 to 12 oranges)
- 6 to 8 cups water
- 2 lemons (preferably 1 regular lemon and 1 Meyer lemon)
- 5 to 6 cups sugar (or more to taste)

## Instructions

1. Scrub the oranges and discard any that are damaged or soft.
2. Cut the oranges in half and juice them, saving the seeds in a separate bowl for making natural pectin.
3. Cut the peels into narrow strips by using a spoon to dig out segment membranes from the inside of the rind and putting some with the seeds for pectin.
4. Use a sharp chef's knife to thinly julienne the peel and combine the cut peels and juice in a large measuring cup.
5. Juice the regular lemon and add this juice to the orange peels and juice, saving the seeds for pectin.
6. Cut the Meyer lemon into eighths lengthwise, remove the seeds and inner membranes, cut the segments into triangular pieces, and add to the measuring cup with the oranges.
7. Add the Meyer lemon seeds to the Seville orange seeds and membranes.
8. Put the citrus seeds and membranes into cheesecloth or a muslin jelly bag and tie tightly.
9. Place the orange and lemon juices and cut peels into a large thick-bottomed pot and add 6 cups of water.
10. Place the cheesecloth or muslin bag containing the citrus seeds and pulp into the pot, submerging it in the liquid and securing the string to the pot handle.
11. Bring mixture to a boil and let boil uncovered for 30 minutes or longer until the peels are completely soft and cooked through.
12. Test the orange peels by taking a bite to ensure they are not firm, adding more water if needed to prevent sticking.
13. Remove from heat and place the pectin bag in a bowl to cool until comfortable to touch.
14. Pour the mixture from the pot into a large measuring cup and measure the amount, then return it to the pan.
15. Add 7/8 cup of sugar for every cup of mixture and stir until dissolved, tasting and adjusting sweetness as desired.
16. Once the pectin bag has cooled, squeeze it like play-doh to extract extra pectin and add about a tablespoon of pectin to the orange mixture.
17. Heat the jelly mixture on medium high and bring it to a rapid boil, stirring occasionally and securing a candy thermometer to the side of the pan.
18. Once the mixture reaches 218°F, start testing by placing a small amount on a chilled plate and looking for wrinkling when pushed with a finger tip.
19. Put several small plates in the freezer to chill and test the marmalade frequently as it approaches setting point.
20. Remove the jelly from heat once it wrinkles on a cold plate.
21. Sterilize the canning jars by placing them in a large pot on a steaming rack, covering with water by 1 inch, and boiling for 10 minutes, then wash the lids in hot soapy water and dry them.
22. Let the jelly sit in the pot for a couple minutes, then carefully ladle the marmalade into the sterilized jars, leaving 1/4 inch head space at the top.
23. Wipe the rim clean with a clean wet paper towel, place the lid on the jar, and secure with a jar ring.
24. Optionally process the jars in a hot water bath for 5 minutes to help get a better seal and prevent mold.
25. Allow the jars to seal on the kitchen counter as the marmalade cools, turning upside down every 30 minutes to prevent the peels from floating to the top.
26. Refrigerate any jars that did not seal and use the marmalade within a few months.
