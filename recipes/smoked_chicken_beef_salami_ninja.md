# Smoked Beef & Poultry Deli Loaf (Ninja Woodfire)

A scaled-up, commercial-grade deli loaf made on the Ninja Woodfire OG701. Cold meat logs absorb smoke before being wrapped and finished low-and-slow — the result is a juicy, perfectly pink deli slice.

## Ingredients

### Meat blend (3 kg total)

- 1,000 g beef, lean or trimmed, cubed
- 1,000 g chicken breast, cubed
- 1,000 g chicken thighs, boneless/skinless, cubed

### Cure & liquids

- 52.5 g fine salt
- 7.5 g curing salt #1 (Prague Powder #1) — mandatory for safety during the long cook
- 15 g sugar or dextrose
- 300 g ice-cold water

### Spice blend

- 6 g garlic powder
- 6 g ground white pepper
- 3 g ground coriander
- 1.5 g ground nutmeg

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Prep and freeze

1. Cut beef, chicken breast, and chicken thighs into 2 cm cubes. Keep beef/breast separate from thighs.
2. Spread cubed meat on baking sheets and freeze 15–20 minutes until edges are firm but not frozen solid.
3. Optional: chill the food processor bowl and blade in the freezer too. Keeping everything under 10°C is critical for a stable emulsion.

### Step 2 — Emulsify (work in batches)

4. Whisk ice-cold water, salt, curing salt, sugar, and all spices together in a pitcher until fully dissolved.
5. Divide into 2–3 identical batches to avoid overworking the food processor motor.
6. Per batch: place cold beef and chicken breast in the processor, pour in half the spiced water, and process on high until the mixture is a very smooth, sticky paste.
7. Add chicken thighs and remaining water. Process just until smooth and uniform — do not over-process; overhandling the thigh fat can break the emulsion.
8. Final texture should be thick, sticky, and hold its shape.

### Step 3 — 24-hour color cure

9. Transfer paste to a large bowl. Press plastic wrap directly onto the surface to eliminate air pockets.
10. Refrigerate 24 hours. This lets the nitrite bond with the meat proteins, developing the signature flavor and deep rosy pink color.

### Step 4 — Cold smoke start

11. Divide chilled paste into 3 equal portions (~1 kg each).
12. With slightly wet hands, shape each into a tight smooth log roughly 7–8 cm in diameter.
13. Fill the Ninja Woodfire pellet hopper with mild pellets (apple, fruitwood, or blend).
14. Place the 3 naked cold logs directly on the cold grill grates side by side.
15. Set dial to SMOKE, temperature to 120°C, and engage the Woodfire Flavor button.
16. Smoke naked for exactly 30–45 minutes. The cold meat will rapidly absorb smoke and develop a golden-pink exterior.

### Step 5 — Wrap and finish low-and-slow

17. Pull logs off the grill. Immediately roll each tightly in heavy-duty plastic wrap, twisting the ends to compress the meat and force out air pockets, then tie off securely.
18. Wrap each plastic-bound log in a layer of aluminum foil to protect the plastic and trap moisture.
19. Set the Ninja to DEHYDRATE at 70°C.
20. Place wrapped rolls on the grate, insert a probe thermometer into the center of the middle roll, and close the lid.
21. Cook until internal temperature reaches 66–68°C (approximately 4–5 hours with the convection fan).

### Step 6 — Ice bath and rest

22. The moment the thermometer hits target temp, pull the rolls out.
23. Submerge immediately in a half-ice, half-water bath for 20–30 minutes to halt cooking and lock in juices.
24. Transfer to the refrigerator, still wrapped, and rest undisturbed for 24 hours.

### Step 7 — Slice and store

25. Unwrap logs, wipe away any residual gelatin on the surface, and slice paper-thin with a sharp knife or meat slicer.

## Notes

- Curing salt #1 is mandatory — the long low-temperature cook requires it for food safety.
- Shelf life: 7–10 days refrigerated; freezes well vacuum-sealed.
- Equipment needed: food processor, heavy-duty plastic wrap, aluminum foil, digital probe thermometer, Ninja Woodfire OG701.
- **Alternative version available**: see `smoked_chicken_beef_salami.md` — a smaller (700 g), home-style variant. It skips the 24-hour color cure and cold-smoke/wrap stages, instead shaping the paste directly and smoking it at 70°C with an optional honey-garlic glaze — a simpler, faster method than this one.
