# Sourdough Croissants

*Source: aberlehome.com*

## Ingredients

- 115g active sourdough starter, 100% hydration
- 36g sugar
- 10g kosher salt
- 29g (2 tablespoons) unsalted butter, melted
- 240g whole milk, cold
- 423g unbleached all-purpose flour
- 227g (1 cup) 82% fat (European-style) unsalted butter, cold
- 8g (1 tablespoon) unbleached all-purpose flour
- 1 egg
- 15g (1 tablespoon) whole milk

## Instructions

1. Feed starter several hours before mixing dough and allow it to peak
2. Sourdough Croissant Dough:
3. 1
4. Day 1: Mix the dough ingredients until a shaggy dough forms
5. Remove dough to a clean countertop and knead for a few minutes until a smooth dough forms
6. Form dough into a ball.
7. 2
8. Transfer dough to a large bowl and cover
9. Leave in a warm place (75°-78°F) to ferment for 4 hours, or until it has nearly doubled in size.
10. 3
11. Cover and refrigerate dough overnight
12. Butter Block:
13. 1
14. Day 2: Place cold butter on a sheet of parchment paper and sprinkle with the flour
15. Fold the ends of the parchment loosely to cover the butter and begin pounding the butter with a rolling pin until it begins to flatten. (Update: I find it's easier to measure and fold the parchment before placing the butter inside.)
16. 2
17. Use a measuring tape to form the parchment paper into a 6x8-inch rectangle pouch over the butter
18. Flip the pouch over to keep the edges closed
19. Then use a rolling pin and your fingers to evenly press the butter into the pouch to make a butter block
20. Chill if butter has begun to melt and looks greasy on the edges
21. If butter is still cold but very pliable when you bend the package, it is ready for the next step
22. Lock in Butter Block:
23. 1
24. Remove dough from fridge and turn it out onto a lightly floured surface
25. Use your hands to begin to shape the dough into a rectangle and pinch the corners to shape them
26. Roll the dough into a 13x9-inch rectangle.
27. 2
28. Unwrap butter block and place in the center of your dough with a short side facing you.
29. 3
30. Bring the ends of the dough over the butter so they meet in the middle of the butter block, encasing the butter
31. Press every seam to seal so the butter does not escape during rolling
32. Brush away any loose flour with a pastry brush
33. First Lamination Fold (Book Fold):
34. 1
35. Turn the dough so the center seam is parallel to you
36. Use your rolling pin to gently pound the dough to lengthen it
37. Then roll to a 24x10-inch rectangle
38. Try to keep the edges and corners straight, and dust underneath with more flour as needed to prevent sticking.
39. 2
40. Fold the short ends of the rectangle inward to meet in the center (Update: meeting the edges together off-center is actually better)
41. Then fold the new ends together like you're closing a book
42. Brush away any loose flour as you go.
43. 3
44. Wrap book in plastic wrap and refrigerate for 20 minutes
45. Second Lamination Fold (Tri-Fold or Letter Fold):
46. 1
47. Place dough on your lightly floured surface again, this time with the fold facing you
48. Pound and roll to a 24x10-inch rectangle again.
49. 2
50. Fold the short ends of the rectangle in thirds this time, as if you are folding a letter
51. Brush away loose flour as you go.
52. 3
53. Wrap in plastic again, and chill for 1 hour
54. Shaping and Baking Croissants:
55. 1
56. Remove dough from fridge and allow it to rest for 20 minutes or until the butter is pliable again
57. With the folded side facing you, roll to a 18x11.5-inch rectangle
58. Use a pizza cutter to trim the edges.
59. 2
60. Cut the rectangle into 5 equal rectangles, measuring for accuracy
61. Cut each rectangle in half on the diagonal to form 10 triangles.
62. 3
63. Set a triangle in front of you with the bottom two corners facing you
64. Brush away loose flour
65. Gently stretch these corners wider with your fingers and then roll the croissant tight enough that there won't be gaps in the finished pastry. (Update: I now make a small cut in the center of the base of each triangle instead of stretching the edges, then roll.) Lightly press the tip of the triangle into the croissant to help it adhere to the underneath side
66. Repeat for remaining triangles.
67. 4
68. Arrange the croissants on 2 parchment-lined half sheet pans, 5 to each pan
69. Cover with plastic wrap (not too tightly) and leave at room temperature to proof for several hours (do not exceed 75°F during proofing), or until croissants have doubled in size and wobble like marshmallows when you bump the pan
70. You should begin to see some separation in the layers when proofing is finished
71. Meanwhile, preheat your oven to 400°F.
72. 5
73. Beat your egg and milk together to make an egg wash
74. Use a pastry brush to brush egg wash over each proofed croissant, avoiding the laminated edges.
75. 6
76. Bake one pan at a time on the center rack for 15-20 minutes, or until deeply golden brown. (Hold second pan in the fridge until you're ready to egg wash and bake if done proofing.)
77. 7
78. Place croissants on a cooling rack, and serve or store once they've cooled completely.
